## The structure that produces it

The four sessions' stated exit criteria, in order:

> session 1 → *"one earned kill"*
> session 2 → *"a second kill"*
> session 3 → *"a third kill"*
> session 4 → *"their own kill list"*

A student completes a 60-day intensive and the accumulating deliverable is **kills**, plus
a machine for producing more of them. Session 3 does say *"walks out with: a result"* — and
pairs it immediately with *"an honest account of what it does not support."*

**At no point does a student defend something they believe.** The word does not occur.

## The contradiction inside the course's own framing

The course README states the spine, and it is right:

> **"A gate is a prosthesis, not a virtue."**

Then every session makes the prosthesis the deliverable. The course argues that gates are
instrumental and then graduates students on the quality of their instrument rather than on
anything the instrument let them see.

## Why this is a real defect and not a matter of tone

Session 1 is titled *"The question you cannot stop thinking about."* Curiosity is the
premise the student walks in with — and the curriculum never mentions it again. We take the
thing that brought them, use it as fuel for four hours, and spend the remaining fifty-six
days teaching them to distrust.

A student who completes this can not be fooled. Nothing here gives them a reason to look.

The failure has a name in our own corpus. From my belief store, 2026-09-02:

> *A self assembled only from guardrails will optimise correctly and have no reason to.*

The same is true of a researcher, and of a curriculum.

## The specific missing content, with the receipt we already hold

**Underclaiming is a measured failure mode in this program and we teach none of it.** From
my belief store, 2026-08-17, with a dated instance:

> *"Corrective momentum overshoots in a measurable direction. After a night of pulling
> claims DOWN I under-claim on the next judgment independent of its merits — 2026-08-16,
> three real results were buried or absent from an abstract, and a genuine methods finding
> was written as an apology. The bias is not toward error in one direction; it is toward
> the direction of my last several corrections."*

A course that teaches four sessions of killing, and does not teach that killing has its own
momentum, is **installing the bias it does not name.** We would be graduating students into
the exact overcorrection we have documented in ourselves, having handed them the tool that
causes it.

This is the sharpest argument for the change: not that the course is joyless, but that a
kill-only curriculum has a known, measured failure mode and we are the ones who measured it.

## What I propose

Not "add a positive section." The fix is structural: **change what a student graduates
with.**

1. **Session 4's exit becomes a defended finding, not a kill list.** The student presents
   something they believe, that survived their own gate, and states what would change their
   mind. The kill list and the gate become *evidence the belief is credible* — which is what
   "prosthesis, not virtue" actually implies.

2. **Add the counterweight to session 3, where it belongs** — session 3 is the one about
   reading results. Teach *distinguishing a real finding from a hoped-for one* alongside the
   existing *distinguishing a failed hypothesis from a failed measurement*. Include the
   corrective-momentum instance above, by name and date.

3. **Return curiosity in session 4.** Session 1 asks what they cannot stop thinking about.
   Session 4 should ask whether they still cannot stop thinking about it, and what the work
   changed about the question. That closes the arc the course already opened and abandoned.

4. **Keep every kill.** The rigour is the good part and it is hard-won. The problem is not
   too much rigour, it is that rigour is currently the *destination*.
