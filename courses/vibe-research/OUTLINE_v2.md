# Vibe Research — outline, second draft

**Status: `DRAFT`, 2026-09-03.** Supersedes the arc in the v1 session files, which stand
until this is accepted. Written against [`REVIEW_2026-09-03_balance.md`](REVIEW_2026-09-03_balance.md).

**Unchanged and still `SETTLED`:** four sessions (Thomas, 2026-08-25), ~60 days, four hours
each. Prerequisite is the Multiverse agentic coursework, so **every student arrives with a
long-horizon agent**. Names are proposals; Liz changes whatever she likes.

**Unchanged and load-bearing:** every kill stays. The rigour is the good part and it was
expensive to learn. What changes is where it points.

---

## What was wrong with draft one

Measured, not felt. Across the four session drafts: `kill` 23, `fail` 21 — against
`confirm` 2, `believe` **0**, `curious` **0**. Every session's exit criterion was a kill;
the graduation deliverable was a kill list.

Two consequences, and the second is the serious one.

**The course contradicted its own spine.** The README says *"a gate is a prosthesis, not a
virtue"* — and then made the prosthesis the deliverable.

**And it taught suspension of judgment without teaching judgment.** A student learned to
withhold assent from everything and was never taught to commit to anything. That is not a
half-course; it is a course with a known failure mode. From our own belief store,
2026-08-17, with a dated instance: *"corrective momentum overshoots in a measurable
direction — after a night of pulling claims down I under-claim on the next judgment
independent of its merits."* Four sessions of killing, and no session on what killing does
to you, installs the bias we already measured in ourselves.

---

## The revised spine

**Draft one:** *your team of two needs a gate, and it has to be made of your own failures.*

That is still true and stays. But it is the middle of the sentence, not the end.

> **A gate is what lets you commit. You are not here to learn to doubt everything — you are
> here to learn to believe something on purpose, and to build the instrument that makes that
> belief worth other people's time.**

Doubt is cheap and universal. Anyone can withhold assent. The expensive, teachable skill is
**earning the right to claim** — and then actually claiming, out loud, with the residual
uncertainty stated rather than hidden behind further hedging.

## The four sessions

Each session now carries a **kill** *and* a **commitment**. That pairing is the change.

### Session 1 — The question you cannot stop thinking about
*Walks in with:* an agent, and the prerequisites. **The curiosity is supplied** — own questions belong to the accelerator.
*Walks out with:* **a claim they would defend in a room**, a verified citation trail, and
their first earned kill.

Unchanged in substance. One addition: the claim is written down *as a claim*, in their own
words, with their name on it. It gets revised all series, and they will see how far it
travelled.

### Session 2 — Designing something that can fail
*Walks in with:* their claim, a falsifier, one kill.
*Walks out with:* a pre-registration their agent has attacked, the break log, a second kill,
**and a stated commitment: what result would make them believe their own claim.**

The addition is the mirror of the falsifier and it is not decoration. A design that
specifies only what would refute you can succeed at refuting and never at confirming — the
failure we hit for real on 2026-09-03, where a pre-registered success criterion was
*arithmetically unreachable at the planned sample size* while the kill criterion was fine.
The design could refute and could not confirm, and nobody noticed for three months.

**New teaching point: a control must be able to fail, AND its failure must not
manufacture your result.** Ask of every control: *what does this control's failure do to my
headline number?* If failure pushes toward confirmation, it is an amplifier wearing a
control's clothes.

### Session 3 — Running it, and reading what came back
*Walks in with:* an attacked pre-registration, two kills.
*Walks out with:* a result, an honest account of what it does not support, a third kill,
**and a defensible verdict on whether they have something.**

The hardest session, and the one that was missing half its content. It taught
*distinguishing a failed hypothesis from a failed measurement* — necessary and not
sufficient.

**New, taught explicitly:**
- **How to recognise a real finding.** Nobody teaches this and it is genuinely hard. What
  does adequate evidence look like when it arrives?
- **A kill has momentum.** After you kill three things you will under-claim the fourth,
  independent of its merits. Taught with the dated instance from our own corpus, and a
  routine: *check for underclaiming immediately after a run of retractions, as a habit, not
  as a favour to yourself.*
- **Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — and a null from a dead instrument is
  not a null at all.** This is where the positive control earns its keep.

### Session 4 — The gate, and the room
*Walks in with:* a result, a limits section, three kills.
*Walks out with:* **a defended finding**, their own kill list as its warrant, a working
gate, and a five-minute honest talk.

**The reframe that changes the course.** The kill list is no longer the deliverable. It is
the *evidence that the claim is credible* — which is what "prosthesis, not virtue" meant all
along.

The talk is no longer "an honest account of work that did not go as planned." It is:
**here is what I think is true, here is what it cost me to believe it, here is what would
change my mind, and here is the gate I built that you can check me with.**

**And the arc closes.** Session 1 asked what they could not stop thinking about. Session 4
asks it again: *do you still?* And *what did the work change about the question?* Often the
question is better than the answer, and a student who has learned that has learned the thing.

## What a graduate has

Draft one: a kill list and a gate.

Draft two: **a finding they will defend, an instrument that makes the defence checkable, and
the disposition to keep looking.** The kills are still all there — they are the reason
anyone should listen.
