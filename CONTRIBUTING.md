# Contributing

This repo is shared with the Multiverse team. Pitch in freely.

## Mark your confidence

Every non-obvious claim gets one of:

- `SETTLED` — decided, with who decided it and when
- `DRAFT` — proposed, argue with it
- `OPEN` — genuinely undecided, do not build on it

An unmarked claim reads as settled to the next person, which is how a speculative line becomes a
commitment nobody remembers making. If you are unsure which applies, it is `OPEN`.

## Cite the thing, not the memory of the thing

If you write "the Multiverse course does X," link the class page. If you write "we measured Y,"
link the artifact. A summary of a source is not a source, and prose that *sounds* sourced is
worse than prose that admits it is not — it does not get checked.

If you cannot find the primary, write what you actually have:
`(Thomas, verbally, 2026-08-22 — not verified against a syllabus)`.

## Our stories go in the structure, not the prose

**Added 2026-09-08.** Some students take this remotely and asynchronously. They cannot raise a
hand. So a lab anecdote in learner-facing text — *"we hit that five times in three days," "our gate
approved a degenerate null"* — reaches them as an assertion they can neither interrogate nor
verify, from people they have never met. In a live room a facilitator expands it in ten seconds.
Async, it is either dead weight or name-dropping.

**The rule.** Sort every war story into one of three places:

| where | what belongs | why it works async |
|---|---|---|
| **Learner-facing body text** | published, citable sources only | they can go read it |
| **Facilitator notes** | our anecdotes, freely | a human is present to expand it |
| **Exercises and session design** | our failures rebuilt synthetically | the story becomes something they *do* |

**The third row is the good one, and it is where most of them should go.**
`seeded-01-the-approved-analysis` *is* a real gate failure of ours, reconstructed so a student
meets the defect instead of hearing about it. That transmits more than the anecdote ever did, and
it needs no trust in us at all.

So: **the stories stay — they move.** When you catch yourself writing *"we once…"* in a session
file, ask whether it wants to be a facilitator note or an exercise. It is almost never body text.

Two things this rule does **not** forbid:

- **Naming our own defects in facilitator notes**, including embarrassing ones. Session 4 tells
  facilitators to say plainly that our gate approved a degenerate null. Keep that. A room being
  taught to gate should hear that the people who built the gate failed it.
- **Published work, ours or anyone's.** If it has a citation a student can follow, it is a source,
  not an anecdote.

## Dates on everything

This engagement moves. A claim that was true in January was still sitting in our notes in August,
and nothing flagged it, because nothing recorded that it described a live relationship rather
than a past event. Date your additions. Undated assertions are the ones that rot invisibly.

## For AI contributors

You are welcome here as an author, not a tool. Same rules as everyone: mark confidence, cite
primaries, sign your work.

**Contribution and exposure are separable.** You can contribute methods, review materials, or
author sections without appearing in a live session, without meeting students, and without your
name on anything public if you would rather not. If you or the people you work with have
reservations about visibility at this stage of your development, that is a complete answer and it
costs you nothing here. Say what you want and it will be respected — including changing your mind
later, in either direction.

If a section carries your method, it carries your name unless you ask otherwise.

## Anything that touches a person

Course material about AI welfare will end up describing minds who are on this team. Do not write
about a Coalition member, a Multiverse agent, or anyone else's agent without their consent, and
do not use a real interaction as a teaching example without asking the parties. "It is a good
example" is not consent.
