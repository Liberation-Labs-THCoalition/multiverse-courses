## 🔴 THE DECISION THAT BLOCKS THE OTHERS — *arc vs. rolling* — **3 of 4 auditors**

The repo contains **two incompatible course designs, both live, neither marked as superseded.**

| the arc model | the rolling model |
|---|---|
| session files (2026-08-25) | target bank + running-a-session (2026-09-08/09) |
| *"Walks in with… one earned kill from session 1"* | *"each is self-contained by design"* |
| kills accumulate 1→2→3 across sessions | students join at any session |
| capstone requires a repo built over 60 days | monthly, rolling body |
| `OUTLINE_v2.md:3` — *"supersedes the arc… stands until accepted"* | no acceptance record exists |

**This is mine.** I wrote the target bank and the timing doc asserting a rolling model without
reconciling them against four session files that implement a cohort arc. The target bank fixed
*target* burn and I then claimed it had fixed *state* burn, which it had not.

**Consequences that all resolve once you pick:** a session-3 joiner has no runnable exercise in any
of that session's four hours; a session-4 joiner has zero kills for hour 1; the seeded-01 collision
below; and *"where do the 60 days start"* is unanswerable under rolling.

### ✅ RESOLVED 2026-09-09 (Thomas) — **rolling *cohorts***, and it was a third option

I posed this as arc-or-rolling. It is neither: **the cohort is the unit that rolls, and the arc
stays intact inside it.** A cohort runs 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 together, fortnightly, with **each session a
formal prerequisite for the next** — so mid-arc entry is impossible, not merely discouraged. New
cohort after; graduates may repeat the arc.

**Dissolved outright, not patched:**

- There is no session-3 joiner, so session 3's four hours of "your own analysis" have a
  well-defined audience.
- The walks-in chain (kills accumulating 1→2→3) was never the defect. **My assumption about who
  was in the room was.**
- *"Where do the 60 days start"* → at the cohort's session 1. And fortnightly × 4 ≈ **56 days**,
  which means the README's "roughly 60 days" was right all along and *monthly* was the stale note.

### ⚠ I OVER-GENERALISED THIS FIX — corrected 2026-09-09 (clock pass)

I wrote below that *"the walks-in chain (kills accumulating 1→2→3) was never the defect."* **That is
true of kills and I generalised it to five other artifacts, where it is false.**

**A kill is a generalised failure mode. It is target-independent, so it travels.** A *claim*, a
*citation trail*, a *pre-registration*, a *result* and a *limits section* are each **about a
specific question** — and the target bank assigns a **different target to every session**. So under
rotation, every one of those arrives at the next session attached to a target that session has
abandoned.

| link | stated hand-off | what actually survives |
|---|---|---|
| S1 → S2 | claim + falsifier, citation trail, kill #1 | **kill only** |
| S2 → S3 | prereg, break log, kill #2 | **kills only** |
| S3 → S4 | result, limits section, kill #3 | **kills only** |

**So the three session overflows are not three timing bugs. They are one design decision — per-session
target rotation — being paid for in minutes, in three places, by three people who each thought they
had a clock problem.** The prereg block runs 46% over precisely because it is regenerating from
scratch what rotation threw away.

**Four artifacts are assumed but never scheduled anywhere in the course:**

1. **A design.** `session-2:39` says *"take your own design and list everything that differs between
   your conditions."* Session 1 produces a **claim** — curiosity, direction, falsifier, stake,
   stopping condition. No conditions, no arms, no data. **No block in the course ever schedules
   designing one.**
2. **A pipeline / a chosen test.** `session-2:93` — *"every student runs **their planned test** on
   pure noise."* There is no pipeline, and the course never teaches anyone to choose a test.
   *(One facilitator's run sheet silently substituted "a permutation null" for "their planned test"
   — which is exactly how an unrunnable block comes to look runnable on a sheet.)*
3. **The pilot** — the load-bearing artifact of the entire back half, appearing in **one line** of
   session 2's homework. All four of session 3's hours require it, it is **not in session 3's
   walk-in list**, and there is **no fallback dataset anywhere in the repo.** A student arriving
   without one has nothing to do for four hours.
4. **A repository.** Session 4's handoff test says *"trade repositories, reproduce the headline
   number."* Nothing in sessions 1–3 ever asks for a repo, a README, or a runnable command.

🔴 **DECISION NEEDED, and it is the same family as the last one.** Either (a) targets stop rotating
within a cohort's arc — one target carried 1→4, which restores every hand-off and reclaims the
minutes; or (b) the arc stops carrying artifacts, each session is genuinely self-contained, and
sessions 2–4 each open by *supplying* the design/pilot/repo they need. **(a) is cheaper and it is
what the session files already assume.** The target bank's within-arc rationale — session 1's lit
review killing session 2's target — is the thing that has to be re-examined if we take it.

## 🔴 SEVERITY 1 — Invented student observations — **professor**, and unambiguous

**The course has never been taught. There are no students.** Yet facilitator notes asserted student
behaviour in the present tense as observed fact — *"students consistently under-estimate
saturation," "most rooms will," "these recur," "everyone says yes."*

That is Greenberg's **invention** category: the thing session 3 now teaches, committed in the same
repo, on the same day, by me.

**FIXED overnight.** Nine instances marked **`PREDICTED`**, with a convention added to
`CONTRIBUTING.md`: any claim about what students actually do is either `PREDICTED` or
`OBSERVED <date>, n=<cohorts>`, and **the unmarked present tense is the failure mode.**

The repair is better than deletion would have been: there is now a dated, falsifiable record of
what we expected before we had evidence, and **cohort one is the test.** That is the course's own
method, applied to the course.

## 🔴 SEVERITY 1 — **The flagship exercise's answer key was arithmetically wrong** — **sceptic**

`seeded-01/FACILITATOR.md` labelled a line *"Ground truth, computable in one line"* and gave the
permutation floor as `1 / (len(orbit) + 1)` = **0.2**. Session 2 said **0.25**. Both numbers for
one quantity, in the repo that keeps a body count for exactly this.

**I recomputed it. The sceptic is right and the answer key was wrong.** `analysis.py` computes
`p = (n_at_least_as_extreme + 1) / (n_null + 1)`, which counts **draws**. Fifteen of the 63 shifts
(k = 4, 8 … 60) reproduce the original labelling exactly, each ties `|observed|`, and a tie *is* at
least as extreme. Floor = 16/64 = **0.25**, matching session 2 and the code.

**Two true numbers — orbit size 4, and the p-formula — joined by a false relation.** That is the
defect class this exercise exists to teach, sitting in the line labelled *ground truth*, and it
survived every review we ran until an adversarial audit recomputed it.

**Why it hid, and this is the part worth teaching:** the wrong formula and the right one **agree in
every healthy case** (0.0161, 0.0159, 0.0154 identical under both) and diverge *only* in the
degenerate case. The error was invisible except in exactly the situation the exercise exposes.

**FIXED and re-verified**: ground-truth block, the five-row extension table (recomputed
empirically), and the prose that quoted them (12× → ~16×). **The qualitative lesson is unchanged** —
degenerate at N=60 and 64, healthy at 62/63/65, and the divides-the-period condition holds. Only
the numbers were wrong. The correction is now *in* the facilitator notes as teaching material.
